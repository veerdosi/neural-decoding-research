## Initial sequence hypotheses

T12's 2023-09-28 attempted-speech data contain 170 three-word utterances over up/right. A reusable neural word pattern might permit reconstruction of mixed sequences after calibration on only repeated-word sequences. Alternatively, neural encoding may depend on position, complete utterance, or planned sequence, so two homogeneous templates could be insufficient.

Predefine two splits before model results: (1) six examples each of right-right-right and up-up-up, holding all six mixed combinations out; (2) three examples each of four even-parity word patterns, holding the four complementary combinations out. All calibration is from the first 85 trials. Small development sets are from that first half but use completely different sequence labels; later eligible trials are reserved for confirmation. No model is supplied with a menu of full utterances, only the vocabulary and labeled temporal examples.

Input is twenty consecutive 200-ms windows of i6v threshold-crossing and spike-power features, 100-4100 ms after go. The source already has author-provided block mean correction; negative mean-centered power is retained, not log transformed. Scaling and twelve-component PCA use calibration trajectories only and no labels. The first representation retains about 50% of calibration variance; full feature comparisons in conventional models will assess information loss.

Both initial Sol calls use text trajectories and no tools. Initial baseline families are position-specific kNN/LDA and whole-trajectory ridge/LDA, with calibration-only CV. These are independent comparison learners; their fitted maps or predictions are never supplied to Sol.

Exact three-word accuracy alone is insufficient to prove order: matching the bag of words can already reach one third on the homogeneous split's mixed combinations. Plan bag-preserving shuffled-recording and temporal-order controls, and report mixed-sequence ordering separately from easier homogeneous cases. No unseen-word, open-vocabulary, or private-inner-speech claim follows from this task alone.

## Discovery-first reassessment, November19

The current positive evidence supports neural-dependent compositional reconstruction and a possible contribution from adaptive inference, but does not establish a robust general advantage over conventional decoding. More tuning of retrieval/DTW would not answer whether another interface to general intelligence is more powerful. Per the user's latest direction, three different mechanisms now take priority over additional marginal validation:

1. **Visual dynamics:** calibration-only PCA16 trajectories rendered as consistent heatmaps;80 sentence examples and eight development queries. Sol sees images, with no numerical retrieval tools in this controlled probe. Hypothesis: visual temporal motifs can support direct multimodal induction. Competing explanation: compression and visual resolution discard useful structure.
2. **Neural alphabet:** calibration-only PCA32 plus64 unlabeled clusters, arbitrary consistently permuted symbols, run lengths and paired whole sentences. Sol performs symbolic induction without retrieval tools. Hypothesis: a discrete representation exposes reusable linguistic structure. Competing explanation: clusters encode task phase or unstable states rather than linguistic units.
3. **Unseen lexical types:** a separate public T12 single-word recording,168 calibration trials over42 word types, with16 queries from eight whole word types absent from calibration. No query menu is supplied; all held-word phonemes occur in calibration words. Sol may use unrestricted general numerical tools and a generic pronunciation dictionary. Hypothesis: phonetic/articulatory structure supports genuinely novel word generation. Competing explanation: four trials per word and whole-word timing do not constrain a transferable neural-to-phonetic relationship.

These are exploratory mechanism probes, not fresh confirmation of the earlier eight T16 queries. The unknown-word trials are new evaluation recordings and new lexical types, but come from one participant/date with chronological recording drift. One meaningful contribution comparison will follow a promising mechanism; extensive validation is reserved for the strongest discovery. Prompts, preparation code and case configurations are in `discovery_probes/` and `code/discovery_probes.py`. No v2 production is underway.

The visual probe completed with no tool use:0/8 exact,24/37 edits (64.86%WER). This representation does not merit a visual-format sweep. It does not establish that all visual interfaces fail.

The neural-alphabet probe also used no tools and produced3/8 exact,9/37 edits (24.32%WER), including the eight-word target "i hope they do not need my family". This warrants a matched comparison and reserved-cohort test. A categorical three-state-per-word HMM, trained by transcript-constrained Viterbi alignment and tuned on the two calibration blocks, produced0/8 exact and20/37 edits (54.05%WER) from exactly the same cluster codes. Its24 calibration-selected settings include one/two/three states, two smoothing strengths, two language-prior weights and two word-transition penalties. This is one meaningful comparator, not evidence that Sol outperforms every conventional sequence model. The baseline family was designed after seeing the Sol development outcome; query labels were not used for its parameter selection. Full32 reserved-cohort HMM predictions are now locked without target access, and a frozen-representation Sol symbol call is running on those32 queries. The independent tool-assisted T16 confirmation is also running. The unknown-word probe remains in progress.
