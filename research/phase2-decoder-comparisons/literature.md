# Literature and scope of novelty

These are primary research sources inspected during Phase 2. They establish neighboring work, not a priority proof for an exhaustive literature search.

- [Kunz et al., Cell 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12360486/) and its [official analysis code](https://github.com/nptl-stanford/inner_speech): the dataset already has supervised word and sentence decoding and shared representations across verbal behaviors. The present contribution cannot be discovering that the words are neurally decodable. The original word analysis uses Gaussian naive Bayes and block mean correction; this motivated the third comparator and drift test.
- [Sano, Amores and Czerwinski, 2024, original version](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07822v1): directly tested zero-shot and in-context GPT variants on numerical EEG features and visual representations for attention and sleep, compared with XGBoost. Conventional models were stronger in their state-detection experiments. Thus black-box LLM interpretation of neural features with labeled context predates this pilot. The arXiv record has a later 2026 revision; the inspected full-text comparison here is explicitly v1.
- [Li et al., RAICL, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17844v1): off-the-shelf vision-language models classify EEG waveform plots with retrieved few-shot examples and domain prompts. It is close training-free precedent, but uses seizure detection and visual EEG rather than numerical intracortical word identification. Its headline findings should not be assumed to transfer to speech or to this model.
- [Nan et al., BrainCoDec, CVPR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08537): meta-trained in-context inference supports adaptation to novel subjects in visual fMRI decoding without target-subject fine-tuning. Training-free adaptation in that work follows specialized meta-training; that differs from using a general-purpose frozen model without neural-specific training in our experiment.
- [Morra et al., NLP4Neuro, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02264v1): uses language-model backbones for zebrafish neural-to-behavior prediction. The implementation includes neural-task fine-tuning/training, so it is not evidence of the same inference-only capability tested here.
- [scikit-learn LDA mathematics and shrinkage documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/lda_qda.html): supports the shared-covariance linear discriminant and covariance regularization used for the independent comparison. Our explicit implementation includes a documented one-example-per-class limit, and matches sklearn fixed-shrinkage predictions in the full-calibration validation check.

The defensible novelty claim must remain narrower than “LLMs can decode brains without training.” A controlled assessment of this particular frontier model on intracortical word recordings, calibration efficiency, drift, batch effects, information-preserving transformations and failure modes could be useful. Any claim that inference-only intelligence is fundamentally better than trained neural decoders requires stronger evidence than a seven-word classification advantage on one representation.
